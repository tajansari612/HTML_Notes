In HTML, headings are used to define the structure and hierarchy of your
web page content. Think of them like the headlines and subheadings in a
newspaper.

HTML offers six levels of headings, from <h1> to <h6>,
with <h1> being the most important and <h6> being the least.

The Heading Hierarchy:

<h1>: The main heading of the page. You should generally only use
one <h1> per page (usually for the title).
<h2>: Major subheadings used to break up the main content sections.
<h3>: Sub-sections under an <h2>.
<h4> to <h6>: Used for deeper, more granular nesting if your
content is highly detailed.

Best Practices for Headings

Don't use headings just to make text bold or big. Use CSS for styling.
Heading should strictly represent the structural meaning of your content.

Stick to sequential order: Don't skip levels.
Avoid jumping from an <h1> straight to an <h3>
It confuses screen readers and disrupts the logical flow.

SEO matters: Search engines (like Google) use headings to understand
what your page is about.Properly structured headings help improve your search rankings.

Accessibility: Visually impaired users rely on screen readers to navigate pages.
Screen readers can read out a list of headings so users can jump straight to the
section they need.
